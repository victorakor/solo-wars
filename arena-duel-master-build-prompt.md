## 1. PRODUCT VISION AND SCOPE

### 1.1 What the product is
A competitive, **online-only 3D shooter** played in the browser and installable on phones as a Progressive Web App. In this phase you build the **1v1 mode**: two players fight in an arena. Each player starts with their own **custom-built weapon**. **Random weapons of different power lie on the floor** and can be picked up during the match. Guns are the core of combat; **melee weapons and throwables are optional** extras the player can choose to use.

### 1.2 The core flow (this is the heart of the product)
1. Player A (the **host**) opens "Challenge," chooses the **match duration** and the **scene (arena)**, and **selects the opponent** (an online friend or an exact username).
2. Player B receives the invite and **accepts or declines**.
3. On accept, both pick a saved loadout (their custom weapon), both clients load the scene, a countdown runs, and the **match begins**.
4. The match runs for the set duration. Whoever has the most kills when time runs out wins (tiebreaks in Section 10).
5. A results screen shows stats, with rematch and back-to-menu options.

"Host" is a **lobby role only**. The host sets rules. The **dedicated server runs the actual game**. Neither player's device is ever authoritative.

### 1.3 Out of scope for this phase (but do not architect against them)
- Story mode, free-for-all/many-player multiplayer, teams, native app-store builds, payments, voice chat, spectator mode.
- **Forward compatibility requirement:** model a match as `MatchConfig { mode, maxPlayers, ... }` with a `participants[]` array and a generic scoreboard. The 1v1 mode sets `maxPlayers = 2`. Do not hardcode "two players" into the room, scoring, or protocol where a list would do. A future many-player mode should need new rules, not a rewrite.

### 1.4 Target players and devices
Players on mid-range and low-end Android phones, iPhones, and desktop browsers, often on limited or metered mobile data and variable latency. This drives the performance and bandwidth budgets in Section 20. The game must be usable on a phone in landscape with touch controls.

## 4. CORE SIMULATION SPEC (shared)

- **Units:** meters, seconds. Y is up. Right-handed (Three.js convention).
- **Server simulation:** fixed 60 Hz step (`dt = 1/60`).
- **Client input:** sampled every simulation step, sent in batches at 30 Hz, each batch carrying the last 3 inputs (redundancy against packet loss). Every input has a monotonically increasing sequence number.
- **Snapshot broadcast:** 20 Hz from server to clients.
- **Client interpolation delay:** 100 ms for remote entities.
- **Lag compensation max rewind:** 250 ms.
- **Reconciliation:** smooth correction if error < 1.5 m, hard snap otherwise. Smoothing should not be visible as rubber-banding during normal play.
- **Collision world:** static geometry is **axis-aligned boxes only** (stairs are stepped boxes). The player is a vertical capsule. Implement capsule-vs-AABB sweep with sliding, ground detection, and auto step-up (max step 0.4 m). Use a simple spatial grid or BVH for broadphase. Collision for rays uses the same box set.
- **Determinism:** the same TypeScript movement function must run on client (prediction) and server (authority) given the same inputs and state.

## 5. PLAYER AND MOVEMENT

### 5.1 Body and hitboxes
- Capsule: radius 0.4 m. Height 1.8 m standing, 1.2 m crouched/rolling. Eye height 1.6 m standing, 1.05 m crouched.
- Hitboxes (used for server hit detection): **head** sphere radius 0.22 m at eye height, **body** capsule radius 0.4 m (the rest).

### 5.2 Movement values (starting)
| Action | Value |
|---|---|
| Walk | 5.0 m/s |
| Sprint | 7.5 m/s (cannot fire or aim while sprinting; short recovery 0.15 s after stopping) |
| Crouch | 2.5 m/s (hold or toggle, configurable) |
| Aim-down-sights (ADS) | movement x0.7 |
| Jump | initial vertical speed 6.5 m/s, gravity 20 m/s² |
| Air control | 30% of ground acceleration |
| Ground acceleration | reach full speed in ~0.12 s, stop in ~0.10 s |
| Stamina | 100 max; sprint drains 20/s; regenerates 15/s after 1 s of not sprinting; must be ≥ 15 to start sprinting |
| Dodge roll | 0.45 s, 5.5 m/s in input direction (or backward if no input), cooldown 2.0 s, cannot fire during roll, body hitbox becomes the crouched height, no invulnerability |

Weapon weight reduces movement speed by the weapon's `moveSpeedMult`.

### 5.3 Health and respawn
- Health 100. No passive regeneration. Healing comes from **medkit pads** (Section 7).
- On death: short fall animation, then respawn after **3 s**.
- **Spawn selection:** choose among the scene's spawn points the one farthest from the opponent that has **no line of sight** to the opponent. If none qualify, choose the farthest.
- **Spawn protection:** 2 s of invulnerability, which ends early if the player fires or throws.
- On respawn the player gets their **custom weapon with a full magazine**, default knife, and 0 throwables. Any floor weapon they carried is lost (see drop-on-death below).
- **Drop on death:** the player's held floor weapon drops as a pickup at the death location with its remaining ammo, and despawns after 15 s. Controlled by a config flag `dropOnDeath` (default `true`).

## 6. WEAPONS, INVENTORY, AND THE CUSTOM WEAPON BUILDER

### 6.1 Inventory slots
| Slot | Contents |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Custom weapon** (the player's own build). Cannot be dropped or lost. **Unlimited reserve ammo**, but magazines require reloading. |
| 2 | **Picked-up firearm** (floor weapon). Limited ammo. Disappears when fully empty, falling back to slot 1. |
| 3 | **Melee.** Everyone has a **Combat Knife** by default. A picked-up melee weapon replaces it, with limited durability; when it breaks, the knife returns. |
| 4 | **Throwable** (frag grenades, max 2 carried). |

Quick-switch with number keys, mouse wheel, or touch buttons. Switching takes the weapon's `equipSec`. A dedicated "quick melee" key performs a melee strike with the slot-3 weapon without permanently switching.

### 6.2 Weapon definition schema (all data-driven)
Every weapon is a config object in `balance.ts`:
`id, name, category (firearm | melee | throwable), fireMode (semi | burst | auto | bolt | pump), damage, headMult, rpm, magSize, reserveAmmo (null = infinite), reloadSec, reloadPerShell (bool), equipSec, spreadHip, spreadAds, adsFovMult, adsSeconds, recoilPitch, recoilYaw, recoilRecovery, hitType (hitscan | projectile), pellets, falloffStart, falloffEnd, minDamageMult, projectileSpeed, projectileGravity, splashRadius, splashDamage, selfDamageMult, moveSpeedMult, durabilityHits (melee), swingSec, hitFrac (fraction of swing when the hit is evaluated), arcDeg, range, rarity, modelId, sfxSet`.

**Rarity tiers** apply to floor weapons: Common (x1.0), Rare (x1.15 damage, +10% magazine), Epic (x1.3 damage, +20% magazine). Roll weights: 60 / 30 / 10. Show rarity by beam and glow color (white / blue / purple).

### 6.4 Custom Weapon Builder
- **Archetypes:** Pistol, SMG, Rifle, Shotgun, Marksman Rifle. Each defines base stats and a theoretical DPS ceiling.
- **Point budget:** 20 points distributed across **Damage, Fire Rate, Magazine, Reload Speed, Accuracy, Range**. Max 8 points per stat.
- **Stat scaling (starting formulas, tunable):** positive stats multiply the base by `1 + 0.06 x points`; reload time multiplies by `1 - 0.04 x points`; accuracy reduces spread by `0.05 x points`.
- **Weight penalty:** heavier builds slow the player: `moveSpeedMult = 1 - 0.004 x (damagePts + magazinePts + rangePts)`.
- **Server-side validation:** the server recomputes the full stat block from the saved build. It rejects builds that exceed the point budget, exceed per-stat caps, or exceed the archetype's DPS ceiling. Clients may only send the build, never the resulting stats.
- **Cosmetics:** body color, trim color, 10 decal presets, weapon nickname (profanity-filtered). Cosmetics never affect stats.
- **Saved loadouts:** up to 5 per account. A live **3D preview** with rotation, a stat bar display, a remaining-points counter, a reset button, and an "equip" button.
- **Lock at match start:** the chosen loadout is validated and frozen when the match enters loading. Changes after that do not apply until the next match.

## 7. FLOOR PICKUPS AND SPAWN SYSTEM

- Each scene defines **8 weapon pads, 2 medkit pads, and 1 power pad**, placed symmetrically.
- **Fair rolls:** the match gets a seed from the server. Mirrored pad pairs receive the **same roll** so both players have symmetrical access. Rolls use the shared seeded RNG; only the server rolls.
- **Timing:** the first drops appear 10 s after the match goes live. After an item is taken, the pad respawns a new roll after 25 s (configurable). Untaken items stay until taken.
- **Weapon pad table (starting weights):** firearms 70% (Sidearm, Rattler, Vanguard, Breaker equal-ish), melee 15%, grenades 15%. Rarity weights as in 6.3.
- **Power pad:** every 60 s, rolls an **Epic** Longshot or Hellfire. Announce 10 s beforehand with an on-screen banner and audio cue showing the location.
- **Medkit pads:** +50 health, respawn after 20 s.
- **Pickup rules:** walking over an item picks it up automatically if the matching slot is empty. If the slot is occupied, show a prompt and require **E** (or a touch button) to swap; the old item drops. Server validates distance (<= 1.5 m), line of access, and ownership race (first valid request wins).
- **Visuals:** bobbing, rotating item with a vertical colored beam, name label when near, subtle pulse when about to spawn.
- **Ammo for floor weapons** is the reserve listed in 6.3 and is **not refilled**. When magazine and reserve are empty, the weapon vanishes.

## 8. COMBAT AND HIT DETECTION (SERVER-AUTHORITATIVE)

### 8.1 Firearms (hitscan)
Client sends: `shotId, slot, clientTick, viewYaw, viewPitch, renderTime` (the interpolated time the shooter was seeing). The server:
1. Validates player state (alive, not rolling, not sprinting, not reloading, weapon equipped, ammo > 0) and **fire rate** (tolerance 10%).
2. Computes the ray origin from the server's player position + eye height, and the direction from the view angles plus **server-generated spread** (deterministic from the match seed and `shotId`; the client uses the same function only for visual effects).
3. **Rewinds** the opponent's hitboxes to `renderTime` (clamped to the last 250 ms of history, using a ring buffer of the last 1 s of hitbox states).
4. Raycasts against static geometry first to find the blocking distance, then against head sphere and body capsule.
5. Applies damage: `base x falloff x (head ? headMult : 1)`. Shotguns evaluate each pellet.
6. Emits events: `hitConfirm` (to shooter), `damageTaken` with direction (to victim), `shotFx` (to both for tracers/impacts), kill feed entry on kill.

### 8.2 Projectiles (rockets, grenades)
Simulated on the server at the sim rate. Clients render them with interpolation and a locally predicted visual for the shooter's own rockets that is corrected by server state. Explosions: splash damage with linear falloff and a **line-of-sight check** from the blast point to the target center (walls block splash). Grenades bounce off boxes with restitution 0.4 and detonate on fuse expiry.

### 8.3 Melee
A swing starts a timer. At `hitFrac` of the swing, the server evaluates a cone (arc and range) from the attacker's eye, using lag compensation like hitscan, and checks line of sight against walls. One hit per swing. Durability decrements on a confirmed hit. Hits cause short hit-stun feedback visually (no control lockout longer than 0.15 s).

### 8.4 Rules
- No friendly fire concept in 1v1.
- Self damage from own splash applies (see weapon table).
- Kill credit goes to the last damager within 5 s (matters for future modes; implement now).
- Track per-player: kills, deaths, damage dealt, damage taken, shots fired, shots hit, headshots, pickups, longest streak.

## 9. NETCODE

- **Transport:** Colyseus over WebSocket. Define all message types in `shared/protocol.ts` with zod validation on the server for every incoming message. Reject and rate-limit malformed messages.
- **Client to server:** `inputBatch`, `fire`, `reload`, `switchSlot`, `pickup`, `drop`, `melee`, `throw`, `dodge`, `ready`, `ping`.
- **Server to client:** `snapshot` (per player: position, velocity, yaw, pitch, state flags, health, stamina bucket, active weapon, ammo, `lastProcessedInput`), plus discrete `events` (shot effects, hits, kills, pickup spawned/taken, projectile spawn/explode, match state changes, announcements).
- **Client-side prediction:** apply inputs immediately, store them, and on each snapshot rewind to the authoritative state and replay unacknowledged inputs.
- **Entity interpolation:** render the opponent about 100 ms in the past between two snapshots; extrapolate up to 100 ms if a snapshot is late, then hold.
- **Clock sync:** ping every 2 s, NTP-style offset estimation, smoothed. Show RTT in the HUD.
- **Bandwidth targets:** < 30 kbps downstream and < 15 kbps upstream per client during normal play. Quantize positions and angles, send only changed fields, and avoid per-tick full-state resends.
- **Connection quality:** warn the player at RTT > 200 ms or > 5% loss. Silence over 3 s triggers a "reconnecting" state; the match **pauses for up to 30 s** awaiting reconnect (with a visible countdown for the other player); after that, forfeit.
- **Reconnection:** use Colyseus reconnection tokens so a refresh or brief network drop resumes the same player in the same match.
- **Debug network simulator:** a client-side debug panel (`?debug=1`) to inject latency (0-500 ms), jitter, and packet loss, used for QA and tests.
- **Server hardening in netcode:** cap buffered inputs per player, reject inputs implying movement faster than allowed, ignore stale sequence numbers.

## 10. MATCH LIFECYCLE AND RULES

### 10.1 State machine
`CHALLENGE_PENDING -> ACCEPTED -> LOADOUT_SELECT (30 s) -> LOADING (20 s timeout) -> COUNTDOWN (5 s) -> LIVE -> [OVERTIME] -> ENDED -> RESULTS`

Define and test every transition, including cancel, decline, expire, disconnect, and timeout paths.

### 10.2 Host settings
- **Scene:** any of the 4 arenas or "Random."
- **Duration:** 3, 5, 7, or 10 minutes (default 5).
- **Optional kill target:** Off, 10, 15, or 25. The match ends early if reached.
- **Floor weapon rate:** Off, Normal, Fast (pad respawn 25 s / 15 s).
- **Opponent:** an online friend from the list, or an exact username lookup. Offline users cannot be challenged.

### 10.3 Invite handling
- The invite arrives as a modal plus notification for the opponent, with accept/decline and a 60 s expiry countdown.
- The host can cancel while pending. If the opponent is in a match, show "busy" and block the invite.
- On accept, the server creates the match room and issues join reservations to both players.
- If either player leaves during loadout select or loading, the match is cancelled and the other player is informed.

### 10.4 Winning
1. Most **kills** when the timer ends (or kill target reached).
2. Tie: most **damage dealt**.
3. Still tied: **sudden-death overtime**, first kill wins, max 90 s. If still tied, declare a draw.

### 10.5 During the match
- Both players are frozen in their spawn areas during the 5 s countdown, with the camera active.
- Timer, score, and kill feed are always visible.
- Leaving mid-match requires a confirmation and counts as a forfeit (a loss).

### 10.6 Results screen
Winner banner, both players' stats (kills, deaths, damage, accuracy, headshots, best streak, pickups), match duration, scene, and buttons: **Rematch** (both must accept; same settings, new seed), **Back to menu**. Friendly matches record stats but do not change ranked rating (Section 17).

## 11. SCENES (ARENAS)

### 11.1 Scene data format (shared JSON)
```
SceneDef {
  id, name, bounds {min, max},
  colliders: [{ type: 'box', pos, size, material }],   // gameplay collision, AABB only
  decor: [ ... visual-only meshes ... ],
  spawnPoints: [{ pos, yaw, mirrorOf }],               // 6 per scene (3 mirrored pairs)
  weaponPads: [{ pos, mirrorOf }],                      // 8
  medkitPads: [{ pos, mirrorOf }],                      // 2
  powerPad: { pos },                                    // 1
  killVolumes: [ ... ],                                 // safety net for out-of-bounds
  lighting {...}, sky {...}, fog {...}, ambience {...}
}
```
The same file feeds the client renderer, the client prediction collision, and the server simulation. Write a **scene validator** (symmetry check, spawn pads not inside geometry, all pads reachable, no unreachable pockets) and run it in CI.

### 11.2 The four arenas
All arenas must be **mirror-symmetric**, fully enclosed (no falling out), with stairs instead of ramps, and have clear sightlines for ranged play, cover for close fights, and routes that let both guns and melee be viable.

1. **Foundry** (industrial interior, about 56 x 40 m): central open floor with container stacks; two catwalk levels along the long sides reached by stairs at all four corners; a central bridge across the middle at 4 m height holding the **power pad**; low crates and pipes as cover. Warm orange sodium lighting, sparks and steam decor.
2. **Sunken Courtyard** (outdoor ruins, about 64 x 64 m): a recessed central plaza (1.5 m down, with stairs), pillar rows, broken walls, side corridors for flanking, a long diagonal sightline for snipers. Overcast daylight, moss and stone palette.
3. **Neon Rooftop** (night city, about 60 x 30 m): two connected rooftops joined by three bridges, AC units and billboards as cover, high parapets all around (no falling), neon signage and light pools. Cool blue/magenta palette.
4. **Dune Outpost** (desert, about 70 x 70 m): terraced dunes, a two-floor central watchtower with the power pad on the top floor, compound walls with gaps, long open range for sniper and rocket play. Dusk lighting with long shadows.

Each arena has 6 spawn points, 8 weapon pads, 2 medkit pads, 1 power pad. Provide a **top-down debug view** (`?debug=1`) showing colliders, pads, spawns, and line-of-sight tests, and use **bot soak tests** to find stuck spots and out-of-bounds exploits.

## 12. ART DIRECTION AND RENDERING

- **Style:** stylized low-poly, flat-shaded materials, limited palette per arena, clean readable silhouettes. No gore: hits show sparks/energy fragments, deaths dissolve into particles after the fall animation.
- **Procedural assets:** build characters, weapons, props, and scenery from primitives and merged geometry with generated materials. Do not depend on hand-authored models. CC0 glTF assets are allowed if licensed and recorded in `CREDITS.md`.
- **Characters:** a procedural humanoid (head, torso, arms, legs) with hierarchical procedural animation: idle, walk/run/sprint blend, crouch, jump, roll, shoot recoil, reload, melee swing, throw, hit flinch, death. Two-tone color customizable from the account's color choice (cosmetic).
- **Weapons:** every weapon in 6.3 has a distinct, readable procedural model for first/third person and for floor pickups.
- **Camera:** **third-person over-the-shoulder** by default with a camera collision check, shoulder swap key, and ADS zoom. Offer a **first-person toggle** in settings (same simulation, different camera).
- **Lighting:** hemisphere + directional light, shadows (soft shadow maps on High, blob shadows on Low), fog, gradient skyboxes. Optional bloom and vignette on High only.
- **VFX:** pooled muzzle flashes, tracers, impact sparks, bullet-hole decals (capped and recycled), explosion particles, grenade trails, screen-space hit direction arcs, damage vignette, rarity beams, pickup pulses.
- **Quality tiers:** Low / Medium / High / **Auto**. Auto measures frame time and adapts resolution scale (0.6-1.0), shadow quality, particle counts, and post effects. Remember the choice.
- **Performance practice:** instancing, geometry merging, shared materials, object pools, zero per-frame allocations in hot paths, frustum culling, and capped draw calls.

## 16. ACCOUNTS, SOCIAL, AND API

### 16.1 Data model (Prisma)
`User(id, username unique, passwordHash, createdAt, avatarId, role, bannedUntil)`, `RefreshToken`, `Friendship(userA, userB, status: pending|accepted|blocked, createdAt)`, `Loadout(id, userId, name, archetype, points json, cosmetics json)`, `Match(id, type: friendly|ranked|bot, sceneId, durationSec, seed, startedAt, endedAt, winnerId nullable, endReason)`, `MatchParticipant(matchId, userId, kills, deaths, damageDealt, damageTaken, shotsFired, shotsHit, headshots, bestStreak, pickups, ratingBefore, ratingAfter)`, `Rating(userId, rating, matchesPlayed, updatedAt)`, `Report(id, reporterId, reportedId, matchId, reason, createdAt, status)`, `AuditLog`.

### 16.2 REST endpoints (all validated with zod)
- Auth: `POST /api/auth/register`, `/login`, `/refresh`, `/logout`; `GET /api/me`.
- Profile: `GET /api/users/:username`, `PATCH /api/me` (avatar, settings sync).
- Friends: `GET /api/friends`, `POST /api/friends/request`, `/accept`, `/decline`, `DELETE /api/friends/:id`, `POST /api/friends/block`.
- Loadouts: `GET/POST/PUT/DELETE /api/loadouts`, `POST /api/loadouts/validate` (returns computed stats).
- Stats: `GET /api/stats/me`, `GET /api/matches/history`, `GET /api/leaderboard?scope=global|weekly|friends`.
- Reports: `POST /api/reports`.
- Admin (role-gated): list reports, ban/unban, view user, server counters.
- Health/metrics: `GET /healthz`, `GET /metrics`.

### 16.3 Realtime social channel
A persistent **presence/notification room** (Colyseus) each logged-in client joins: friend online status, incoming challenge invites, friend requests, match readiness, and system notices. Challenge messages: `challenge.create`, `challenge.accept`, `challenge.decline`, `challenge.cancel`, `challenge.expire`. On accept, the server creates the match room and returns join reservations.

### 16.4 Account rules and security
- Username 3-16 chars `[a-z0-9_]`, unique, case-insensitive, profanity filtered. Password minimum 8 characters, argon2id. Optional email field with verification and password reset flow stubbed behind a mail-adapter interface (console adapter for dev, SMTP adapter configurable).
- Rate limits: auth 10 requests/min/IP, friend requests 30/hour, challenges 20/hour, general API 120/min.
- Helmet, strict CORS allowlist, HttpOnly + SameSite refresh cookie, short-lived access tokens, refresh rotation, token revocation on logout.

## 17. RANKED QUICK MATCH, STATS, AND PRACTICE MODE

- **Ranked Quick Match:** a queue that pairs random opponents by Elo (start 1000, K=32). Band starts at +/-100 and widens by 25 every 10 s up to +/-400. **Fixed rules:** 5 minutes, floor weapons Normal, random scene from the pool, kill target Off. Loadouts lock at match start. Elo updates on completion; **forfeit counts as a loss**. Friendly challenges never change Elo.
- **Leaderboards:** global top 100 by rating (minimum 10 ranked matches), weekly wins, and friends-only.
- **Practice vs Bot:** a server-run match against an AI opponent with Easy/Medium/Hard. The bot uses the same input pipeline as a human (aims with human-like reaction delay and error, picks up weapons, uses melee at close range, throws grenades). It exists for solo practice and is also the main automated-testing opponent. Practice matches never affect ranking.

## 18. SECURITY AND ANTI-CHEAT

- Server authority on all game state; validate every message's schema, size, and rate.
- Validate movement: reject speeds beyond limits, detect teleports, clamp to scene bounds.
- Validate fire rate, ammo, reload, state, and weapon ownership on every action.
- Validate custom weapon builds on the server; never trust client-sent stats.
- **Heuristic cheat flags (log and flag, do not auto-ban):** inhuman angular snap speeds, sustained headshot ratios far above norms, reaction times below human limits. Surface flags in the admin view.
- Report button on the results screen, admin tools to review reports, temporary and permanent bans, audit log.
- Document known limitations honestly in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` (for example, 1v1 needs opponent positions on the client, so wallhack-style cheating is possible).
- Dependency audit in CI. TLS required in production. No secrets in client bundles.

## 20. PERFORMANCE BUDGETS (enforce in CI where possible)

- Frame rate: 60 fps on a mid-range integrated GPU desktop; **>= 30 fps on a 2019 mid-range Android phone at the Low preset**.
- Memory: < 400 MB in a live match on mobile.
- Network: see Section 9 targets. Provide a **Low-data mode** (smaller particle/audio loading, reduced non-essential events).
- Battery saver option (30 fps cap, reduced effects).
